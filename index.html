Abstract

The reliability of autonomous systems in real-world environments is mainly dependent on the robustness of their visual perception. Although recent studies have advanced the handling of visual degradations, physical contaminants that adhere to the camera lens, such as mud, water droplets, and condensation, remain largely underexplored. To this end, we introduce the CLP (Contaminated Lens Protector) dataset, a real-world benchmark designed to evaluate perception performance under realistic lens-protector contamination. The CLP dataset offers degraded images across multiple types of contamination and various lens-to-protector distances, along with dense semantic segmentation masks and aligned restoration targets.

Dataset

Overview CLP captures real lens-protector contamination using a custom 3D-printed smartphone holder that fixes glass plates at four precise distances in front of the camera lens.
  • 3 contamination types: mud, water droplets, condensation
  • 4 lens-to-protector distances: 0, 1, 3, 5 cm
  • Paired clean & degraded images with dense semantic labels

600 scenes captured across diverse indoor/outdoor environments, day/night, and three camera angles (up, horizontal, down).
  • 4,800 degraded images (600 scenes × 2 refs × 4 distances)
  • 125 semantic classes, 19,203 annotated instances
  • Train / Test split: 480 / 120 scenes

Experiments

Quantitative Results We benchmark 12 models across segmentation and restoration tasks. DINOv2 achieves the best overall mIoU (47.2%), while DiffUIR leads restoration with 27.92 dB PSNR. Domain Generalization methods consistently outperform Domain Adaptation despite having no access to contaminated training data.

Qualitative Results Segmentation quality degrades significantly at larger distances due to increased blur and occlusion. DINOv2 maintains coarse semantic structures even at 5 cm, while most other models fail under severe mud contamination. DiffUIR produces the best visual restoration but visible artifacts remain in heavily degraded regions.

Conclusion

Conclusion CLP provides a real-world benchmark for robust perception under realistic lens-protector contamination, offering paired images, dense semantic labels, and multi-distance captures. Our experiments establish diverse baselines for segmentation and restoration, revealing that domain generalization and foundation model pretraining are key to robustness.
Limitation / Future Work Current captures are limited to smartphone cameras; extending to automotive or surveillance setups remains future work. Data scaling experiments show diminishing returns, suggesting more diverse scene collection could be beneficial. Task-aware restoration is a promising direction for improving perception under contamination.
